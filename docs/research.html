Research

Expectations, forecasts, and economic decisions

My current research centers on economic expectations and forecasting, with particular attention to how forecasts are formed, evaluated, and communicated.

Working Papers

Evaluation of Population Forecasts in Japan and Their Implications for Low Fertility and Population Aging

This project evaluates population forecasts in Japan and studies their implications for understanding low fertility and population aging.

Work in Progress

How Firms Form Expectation: Evidence from Plant-Level Production Forecasts

A project on firms’ expectation formation using plant-level production forecasts.

When Numbers and Narratives Diverge: Assessing the Consistency of Economic Forecast Communication

A project examining consistency between numerical forecasts and narrative communication.

What Do the Text Data from Consumer Sentiment Surveys Tell Us?

With M. Kawagoe. A project using text responses from consumer sentiment surveys to study the information contained in households’ explanations of their economic outlook.

Stylizing the long-term macroeconomic forecasts

A project documenting empirical patterns in long-term macroeconomic forecasts.

Research Themes

Economic forecasting and forecast evaluation. Accuracy, rationality, asymmetric loss, information rigidity, disagreement, and the value of point, range, and interval forecasts.

Expectations and beliefs. Expectations of firms, households, professional forecasters, governments, central banks, and international institutions.

Applied econometrics. Empirical methods for evaluating forecasts, expectations, and survey information.
